# Research Sub-Circles Charter (template)

Part of the [Research Circle Pattern](../pattern.md). A template for chartering a research sub-circle; it may be adjusted as needed. No sub-circle is known to have been chartered yet (guess).



### 0. Code of practice

At Life Itself, governance is not a constraint on creativity but its **container**. When we organise with care, we create the safety and clarity that let inquiry deepen, so that thinking can become collective and collaboration stay generative.

Sub-circles are not hierarchies of expertise but **ecosystems of relationship**. They need balance: freedom to explore and structure to sustain trust. This code of practice makes that balance visible and shared. Governance is not bureaucracy; it is a form of **relational hygiene**, the way we tend the conditions of our work so that our inquiry reflects the integrity, compassion and systemic intelligence we wish to see in the world. It is always incomplete, and works within a wider culture and set of norms that are not fully codified (and cannot be).

### 1. Orientation and purpose

A **Research (Sub)Circle** is a self-organizing community of inquiry exploring a specific dimension of regenerative culture, systems thinking and institutional design, from metacrisis towards a Second Renaissance. Each combines scholarship, experimentation and reflection to produce open-access knowledge and embodied insight.

**Purpose and duration**

- To explore a specific question or theme over (usually) a **6–9 month cycle**.
- To generate tangible outputs (publications, dialogues, prototypes) while cultivating collective learning.
- To operate as a *microcosm* of Life Itself's mission: modelling regenerative governance and culture through lived experience.

**Connection to the Research Circle and Life Itself.** Sub-circles are part of the Research Domain of Life Itself, feeding insight into programmes, partnerships and organisational development. They are how Life Itself learns about itself, by studying, reflecting and evolving in community.

**Deliverables and outputs** (examples)

| Output type | Description | License |
|---|---|---|
| Research note series | 3–5 short thematic essays (3–5k words) | CC-BY |
| Longform paper / report | Integrative synthesis for publication | CC-BY-SA |
| Repository | Living knowledge base with references and notes | CC0 |
| Open symposium | Live event or online panel showcasing findings | CC-BY-NC |
| Ecosystem map | Visual map connecting key domains and thinkers | CC-BY-SA |

**Design principles for outputs.** Rigor (academically sound) and accessibility (readable by practitioners and policymakers). Outputs should bridge the two, though any piece may lean one way.

**Tools and infrastructure**

- Workspace: Google Docs and/or Markdown knowledge bases (MOF: markdown, Obsidian, Flowershow)
- Discussion: WhatsApp (coordination), Forum (public reflections)
- Documentation: Google Drive + Zotero (research library)
- Publication: Substack / research website
- Governance: Loomio (consent-based decisions), OpenBooks (budget transparency)

### 2. Membership framework

- Membership is by **invitation or open call**, approved by the sub-circle's Stewardship Circle.
- Minimum: **two sessions per month** of active contribution.
- Members record brief updates in a shared log after absences.
- Re-entry after more than 2 months requires a re-alignment conversation and the circle's consent.

### 3. Cultural ground

**Behavioural agreements**

1. Listen before responding.
2. Engage differences as resources, not obstacles.
3. Practise reciprocity: give as you receive.
4. Speak from "I", not from assumption.
5. Value precision and kindness equally.

**Anti-extraction statement.** "We learn *with*, not *from*." Research at Life Itself is not extractive. We do not mine ideas or relationships for output; we **co-create knowledge as commons**. Authorship, credit and participation honour contribution, not control.

**Energetic hygiene**

- **Pause** when overwhelm, tension or confusion arises.
- **Name** what you sense without accusation.
- **Reflect** before reacting; document observations privately if needed.
- **Re-ground** through a short check-in or silent minute before continuing.

### 4. Decision and power dynamics

- All decisions are made by **consent** ("no reasoned and paramount objection").
- Authority is **functional, not positional**: roles serve the circle, not the person.
- Each circle operates semi-autonomously within Life Itself's governance framework.

**Leadership rotation.** Facilitation and stewardship roles rotate **every 3 months**, or sooner by consent if energy becomes unbalanced. Rotation is renewal, not critique.

**Extended absence and interim stewardship.** Absence beyond **8 weeks** without notice triggers review. The circle may appoint an **interim steward** by majority consent. The returning member rejoins after a re-alignment dialogue with a facilitator and two peers.

### 5. Conflict transformation

Conflict is natural in inquiry. What matters is whether we metabolise it into growth or toxicity. Handle tensions with **clarity, confidentiality and compassion**.

1. **Early: reflection and documentation.** Notice the tension; reflect privately and note it briefly in the shared *Circle Log*; request a 1-on-1 reflective dialogue, ideally with a peer present.
2. **Mid: restorative triad.** If it persists, form a triad (neutral peer, facilitator, the parties). Hold a restorative dialogue (up to 90 minutes), write a short summary of needs, impacts and agreements, and report back to the circle (summary only).
3. **Escalation: stewardship review.** If trust keeps eroding, the circle requests mediation by the Stewardship Circle or an external facilitator. Stewardship may suspend or reconfigure a role. After repeated violations, membership may be **revoked by consensus minus one**.

Guiding principles: confidentiality (what is shared in process stays in process), compassion (assume good intent, name real impact), clarity (document agreements and follow up).

### 6. Authorship and ethics

- Primary credit: "The [Circle Name], Life Itself Research Collective." Individual authors are listed alphabetically or by major contribution. Extended absence (over 3 months) reverts to "acknowledgment" rather than lead authorship.
- Public talks or interviews representing the circle need the consent of **two peers**. Media statements must align with shared findings.

| Type | License | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| Text and publications | CC-BY 4.0 | Open use with attribution |
| Co-created works | CC-BY-SA 4.0 | Share-alike, protects commons |
| Data and notes | CC0 | Public domain |
| Visuals / media | CC-BY-NC 4.0 | Credit required, non-commercial use |

### 7. Renewal and reflection

**Quarterly re-alignment ritual.** At the close of each quarter: read aloud the Circle Charter; each member names one commitment and one tension; update OKRs and learning goals; share one "harvest insight".

**Collective evaluation.** Focus on *process quality*, not personal performance. Members reflect on cultural health (trust, clarity, curiosity, coherence). Adjustments are made collectively and recorded.

**Role and health review.** Rotations or sabbaticals are decided by consent. Any emerging pattern of power imbalance is discussed openly.

### Appendix: ethical and academic standards

- All works are published under open licenses (CC-BY or CC-BY-SA).
- Citation and attribution follow academic standards (APA or MLA).
- Plurality of perspectives is prioritised; no single ideology is privileged.
- Psychological and cultural safety norms are observed.
- Consent is required for recording or quoting live discussions.

### Appendix: quarterly reflection and evaluation

Every 3 months the circle reviews: What have we learned collectively? What patterns are emerging? How do these insights evolve our practice? How has participation changed individual and collective awareness?

### Appendix: end-of-cycle review may include

- Learning report (5–7 pages)
- Partner feedback synthesis
- Proposal for next iteration (topics, participants, funding)
