# Research group goal: options and debate

These notes come from the Goal Tree session on 2026-10-22, with Rufus, Bob Bunge and Jonah Wilberg. They are kept apart from the [reasoning state](reason-commons.yaml) because this debate is likely to come up again.

## The question

Above the group's goal sits the second renaissance itself: a civilisational transition. So the question is: **how does a research group contribute to that transition?** That is our theory of change.

## Two separate choices

1. **Explore or commit.** We could leave it open: the goal is to work out how research helps a second renaissance. Or we could commit to one theory of change. The open option is meta and gives us nothing to fail against.
2. **Order.** Rufus compared this to money and mission in a company. Both are needed, but one is the goal and the other a success factor. Here, the order question is between outputs (papers, essays) and collective sensemaking (the conversation).
   - Rufus, and the 2025 framing: outputs come first, and sensemaking supports them.
   - Bob: "the conversation is the cognition" (after Peirce and Habermas). Collective cognition is the main output, and artifacts are needed so it doesn't "disappear like tears in rain".
   - With little volunteer time, this becomes a real conflict between the two.

## Options for the goal

| | Option | Gist |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Explore** | Find out how a research group best contributes to a second renaissance. |
| B | **Instruct** | Produce knowledge that people building the transition can use, for example how paradigm shifts happen. Rufus's "rocket engineering". |
| C | **Seed** | Clarify paradigms (today's, their genealogy, emerging ones, how transitions happen) and spread that clarity into the culture. The Frankfurt School both analysed and shifted the culture. This is Bob's Meadows leverage point 2, the paradigm out of which the system arises. |
| D | **Embody** | Be a living example of second renaissance sensemaking: collective cognition that is not purely rational, joining doing and being. |
| E | **Home** | A place for Seeds of Renaissance community members who are drawn to research to gather, learn and contribute: the "deeper" step in the SoR funnel. |

Bob's reading of Meadows: point 1 (transcending paradigms) is inner work that happens in individuals. The group can inspire that work but not do it. Point 2, clarifying paradigms, is "the sweet spot". Points 3 and below are implementation, where the artifacts appear.

## Test for the top spot

Ask of each option: "If only X happened this year, would we call it a success?"

- **D alone fails.** Great conversations that leave nothing behind; Bob agrees.
- **B alone fails.** Good papers that nobody reads.
- **E alone** is a success for the community, but not for research.
- **C passes.** It needs B as its content, D as its method, and some reach.

## Decided (2026-10-22)

**Goal = C.** Rufus and Bob agreed. B, D, seeding and E become success factors under it. A becomes a stance, not the goal: hold C as a working bet and review it at the quarterly reflection.

Working wording: *The group clarifies the paradigm shift (what today's paradigm is, where it came from, what is emerging and how transitions happen) and seeds that clarity into the SoR community and the wider culture.*

## Open: "and the wider culture"

**Jonah's critique.** Up to "the SoR community", the goal is specific. "And the wider culture" is a big leap where "the magic happens", and it hasn't been clarified. He wants the goal to name who in the wider culture we focus on first (policy people, the general public, …). That audience then decides the means of transmission, for example policy papers if the audience is policy people.

**Rufus's answer.** It is partly a black box, but there are known recipes. Effective altruism started as a few people at Oxford mostly talking to each other, and spread over 10–15 years with funding and memes. This is why the group writes papers, videos and memes and doesn't only talk: artifacts travel further than conversation. He would accept a tweak along the lines of "the wider culture, starting with …".

**Bob's answer.** Choosing an audience and means of transmission is "too downstream" for the goal.

**Question:** does the audience need to be in the goal now?

## If E is what's really wanted

E is a legitimate goal, but for a different thing: an SoR research-interest track, in which research is a means to belonging. If we ever choose it, choose it explicitly rather than letting it creep in.

## The output-vs-conversation conflict (an Evaporating Cloud to run later)

- **Shared objective:** shift the paradigm.
- **Rufus's side:** the ideas must last beyond the room, so the group needs finished, published outputs.
- **Bob's side:** the group must achieve real collective cognition, so live dialogue comes first.
- **Candidate way out:** harvest the conversations themselves. Turn each session into a short written piece and post it on the forum. The stewards' decision on 17 Sep to post a forum stub for each session is a weak version of this. If harvesting is cheap, the conflict largely goes away.
